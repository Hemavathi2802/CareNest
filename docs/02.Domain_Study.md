# Domain Study

## 1. Domain
**Home healthcare service coordination** — arranging nurse visits at patients' homes and recording the care associated with those visits.

## 2. Domain Overview
Home healthcare supports people who need nursing care at home, including older adults, people recovering after surgery, and people who have difficulty travelling to a care facility. The service coordinates patient requests, nurse availability, visit scheduling, care notes, and communication about appointment status.

This application supports those coordination workflows. It is not a replacement for a hospital information system, emergency service, or professional clinical decision-making.

## 3. Stakeholders and Responsibilities
- **Patient:** registers, maintains a patient profile, requests and tracks appointments, and views their schedule, notifications, medical records, and visit reports.
- **Nurse:** maintains a nurse profile and availability, reviews assigned appointments, records patient-care information, and submits visit reports.
- **Administrator:** manages patient and nurse information and oversees appointments, schedules, availability, and visit reports.

## 4. Domain Workflows

### Patient appointment
1. A patient creates an account and provides the information needed for a patient profile.
2. The patient requests a home-care appointment by selecting a service, date, and time.
3. The appointment is associated with the patient's profile identifier, which is distinct from the account's user identifier.
4. The patient views appointment status and may cancel the request.
5. A nurse reviews assigned appointments and accepts or rejects them. Appointment and reminder communications may be sent through configured email and in-app notifications.

### Nurse visit and care record
1. A nurse maintains availability and reviews their schedule and assigned patients.
2. During or after a visit, the nurse records patient-care information, which can include observations such as blood pressure, temperature, pulse, and oxygen saturation.
3. The nurse submits a visit report associated with the appointment, patient, and nurse.
4. The patient and administrator can review the information exposed to their respective application screens.

### Administrative oversight
1. The administrator reviews patient, nurse, and appointment information.
2. The administrator manages operational schedules and nurse availability.
3. The administrator reviews visit reports to monitor completed care.

## 5. Core Domain Concepts
- **User account:** login identity and role; a user account is not itself a patient profile.
- **Patient profile:** patient-specific contact and demographic information; identified by `patient_id` and linked to its account by `user_id`.
- **Nurse profile:** nurse-specific contact, specialization, experience, and availability information linked to a user account.
- **Appointment:** a patient's request for a named care service at a date and time, with an optional nurse, notes, status, and reminder state.
- **Nurse availability:** the days and time range when a nurse can be scheduled.
- **Schedule:** appointment information presented for a patient, nurse, or administrator.
- **Patient-care record:** visit-linked care data, including recorded vital signs, care notes, and a possible next visit.
- **Visit report:** symptoms, observations, treatment, medicines, and notes associated with an appointment, patient, and nurse.
- **Medical record:** patient medical history, diagnosis, medicines, allergies, and notes. Its creation timestamp is separate from a visit date.
- **Notification:** patient- or nurse-facing appointment/reminder message with read state and creation time.

## 6. Domain Rules and Data Considerations
- Appointment patient references must use the patient profile ID, not the user account ID.
- A visit report is linked to the appointment, patient, and nurse involved in the visit.
- Appointment status communicates workflow progress; it must not be confused with a clinical outcome.
- Date and time shown in patient and nurse communications should be unambiguous and include the appropriate AM/PM indicator where displayed in 12-hour format.
- Medical records and visit reports contain sensitive health information and must only be accessible to authorized roles and users.
- Persist only the health and identity information required for care coordination; do not expose credentials or unnecessary personal data in API responses.

## 7. Current Application Scope
- Patient registration, sign-in, email OTP flows, and password reset.
- Patient and nurse profiles and nurse availability.
- Appointment requests, status actions, schedules, and notifications.
- Patient-care entries, medical records, and visit reports.
- Administrator views for managing patients, nurses, appointments, schedules, availability, and reports.

## 8. Out of Scope
- Emergency response, ambulance dispatch, or urgent triage.
- Diagnosis recommendations or automated clinical decisions.
- Electronic prescribing or pharmacy fulfilment.
- Online payments, insurance claims, or billing.
- Video consultation and laboratory booking.
- Integration with external electronic health record systems, unless added as a separately defined requirement.

## 9. Domain Risks and Open Decisions
- Access control for patient health information must be enforced by the backend, not only by hiding frontend pages. The current backend security configuration permits all requests and must be corrected before public deployment.
- Nurse assignment rules, appointment conflict handling, service durations, cancellation windows, and notification retry behavior need explicit business decisions before being treated as guaranteed system behavior.
- Health-data retention, consent, audit logging, backup, and recovery requirements must be agreed before production use.
